# Worked examples across niches

Every "before" here is real copy of the kind that gets written by default. The
diagnosis matters more than the rewrite, because the diagnosis is what
transfers to the next piece.

---

## Ecommerce, high consideration

**Before:** "Premium cedar barrel sauna. Seats 6. Authorized dealer. Free
shipping to the lower 48. 30-day returns."

**Diagnosis:** Warm-stage copy at a cold audience. Five facts, no moment.
"Premium" is a claim the reader has no reason to accept. Everything here is
persuasive to someone comparing three sellers and invisible to someone who has
not decided they want a sauna.

**After:** "Some of the best conversations happen at 180 degrees. We put them
in back yards."

**What changed:** One driver (belonging), one moment, a turn. The shipping and
returns move to the product page where someone is actually deciding.

## B2B service

**Before:** "We help small businesses leverage AI automation to streamline
operations and drive efficiency at scale."

**Diagnosis:** Zero specifics, three buzzwords, could be any of 10,000
companies. No moment, no person. "Small businesses" is a market.

**After:** "Your phone rang at 7pm on Saturday and somebody answered it. It was
not you."

**What changed:** Relief driver. A specific time, a specific event, and the
payoff is in the last three words. The reader supplies the rest.

## Local trade

**Before:** "Family-owned HVAC company serving the greater Los Angeles area for
over 20 years. Licensed, bonded and insured. Free estimates."

**Diagnosis:** Credentials before wanting. Every competitor says the same five
things, so it differentiates nothing. "Over 20 years" is a rounded estimate.

**After:** "It is August, it is 104 outside, and the upstairs bedroom is
usable. We have not missed a same-day call in two summers."

**What changed:** Relief driver, a real moment, and the credibility claim is
now a specific that only a real operator could make. Licensing still belongs on
the site, further down, where it reassures rather than bores.

## Nonprofit

**Before:** "We provide comprehensive support services and life skills training
to empower foster youth to achieve successful outcomes."

**Diagnosis:** Institutional language. "Empower", "outcomes", "comprehensive".
Describes a programme, not a person. Nobody donates to a programme.

**After:** "He is seventeen and nobody has ever shown him how to open a bank
account. Thursday, somebody will."

**What changed:** Identity driver for the donor, one real person, a specific
unglamorous task, and a time. "Somebody will" leaves room for the reader to
become that somebody.

Note the restraint. No tragedy, no pity, no stock phrases about breaking
cycles. Dignity is more persuasive than pathos and it does not curdle on the
second reading.

## SaaS

**Before:** "Our platform reduces month-end close time by up to 40% with
automated reconciliation and real-time reporting."

**Diagnosis:** "Up to" is a tell that the number is a ceiling nobody hits.
Features listed, benefit asserted, nothing seen.

**After:** "Your controller went home at six on the last day of the quarter."

**What changed:** Control driver. One person, one specific day, one image. The
40% claim belongs on the product page with a source attached.

## Restaurant

**Before:** "Authentic Italian cuisine crafted with the finest locally sourced
ingredients in a warm, inviting atmosphere."

**Diagnosis:** Every word is a category. "Authentic", "finest", "warm and
inviting" are what the restaurant next door also says. No dish, no person, no
hour.

**After:** "The pasta is made at 6am by a woman who will tell you if you order
wrong."

**What changed:** Pleasure and belonging. A specific time, a specific person,
and a small flaw that reads as character. The flaw is what makes it believable.

## Real estate

**Before:** "We buy houses fast for cash. No repairs needed. Close in as little
as 7 days."

**Diagnosis:** This is everyone's copy, which means it reads as a sign stapled
to a telephone pole. The offer is fine, the credibility is zero.

**After:** "You do not have to clean it out. You do not have to fix the roof.
Tell me what you need to walk away with and I will tell you today if I can do
it."

**What changed:** Control driver. Still two negatives, which is the limit, and
they earn their place because removing obligations is the actual offer here.
The turn is the last clause: a same-day yes or no is a specific promise, and
"if I can do it" is the honesty that makes the rest credible.

## Agency or consultant

**Before:** "We are a full-service digital marketing agency delivering
data-driven results for ambitious brands."

**Diagnosis:** Says nothing. "Full-service" means no specialism, "data-driven"
is table stakes, "ambitious brands" flatters the reader instead of describing
them.

**After:** "We are not the cheapest and we will not take you on if we do not
think we can move the number. Three of our last five clients came from people
we turned down."

**What changed:** Authority through refusal. The admitted limit buys the claim,
and the specific ratio is the proof. This only works if it is true, which is
the point.
