# Desire drivers: finding the one

Copy aimed at three drivers hits none. This is how to pick the one, and what
each sounds like when written well and badly.

## How to find it

Ask the question the buyer would never say out loud.

Not "why do people buy a home sauna" but "what is true about their life after
they have one that is not true now". The answer is rarely the product
category.

Three ways to get there:

1. **The last straw.** What happened the week before they started looking?
   Nobody researches a product in a vacuum. Something happened. That thing is
   the driver.
2. **The thing they tell their spouse.** The justification they use out loud is
   usually the rational cover. The real reason is underneath it, and the copy
   should speak to the real one while giving them the cover to repeat.
3. **What would embarrass them to admit.** Status, envy, fear of being seen as
   cheap, wanting to be the kind of person who does this. These drive enormous
   amounts of spending and almost never appear in the brief.

---

## Relief

**The wanting:** make the bad thing stop.

Strongest where the current state is actively unpleasant and the reader is
already aware of it. You do not need to create the problem, only to show you
understand it from the inside.

**Written well:** the problem described so accurately the reader feels caught,
then the quiet after. Understatement beats intensity. The reader already knows
how bad it is, so matching their volume rather than exceeding it is what
signals you have been there.

> "The house is quiet again and nobody is talking about the upstairs bedroom."

**Written badly:** amplifying pain the reader already feels. "Are you TIRED of
sweltering summers?" insults them. They know. Manufactured agitation reads as
manipulation because it is.

---

## Identity

**The wanting:** become, or confirm you already are, a certain kind of person.

The most powerful and most easily overplayed. Works by showing the person the
reader wants to be, doing the thing, **unremarkably**. The unremarkableness is
the trick. Aspiration presented as ordinary is inviting. Aspiration presented
as extraordinary is exclusionary.

**Written well:**

> "You do not have to be extraordinary. You have to be there on Thursday."

**Written badly:** "Join the elite few who..." Anything that makes the reader
audition. Also anything that tells them who they are: "You are a driven,
ambitious professional who..." is presumptuous and they can feel it.

---

## Belonging

**The wanting:** be with these people, or be like them.

Needs other people in the scene. Most copy is weirdly empty of humans, which is
why so much of it feels sterile.

**Written well:**

> "There are eleven other people in the room who said yes too."
> "Some of the best conversations happen at 180 degrees."

**Written badly:** "Join our community of 50,000+ users." A number is not a
belonging, it is a statistic. Belonging is two people in a room, not fifty
thousand in a database.

---

## Control

**The wanting:** stop being at the mercy of something.

Underrated, and enormous in B2B, finance, health, home and anything with a
recurring unpleasant surprise. The emotional payload is the difference between
knowing and not knowing.

**Written well:** contrast the moment of knowing against the not-knowing
before it.

> "You will know what you owe in January, not April."
> "Tell me what you need to walk away with and I will tell you today."

**Written badly:** "Take control of your finances." A slogan, not a moment.
Control copy lives or dies on the specific thing that stops being uncertain.

---

## Pleasure

**The wanting:** they just want it.

The only driver that needs no justification, and the one most often ruined by
supplying one. Sensory, direct, unapologetic. The instant you explain why the
pleasure is deserved or beneficial, you have turned it into a justification and
killed it.

**Written well:**

> "The pasta is made at 6am by a woman who will tell you if you order wrong."

**Written badly:** "Indulge in a well-deserved moment of self-care." Three
clichés and a permission slip. Nobody needs permission, and offering it implies
they do.

---

## Mixing

One dominant driver per piece. A second can appear underneath if it does not
compete for the same sentence.

The usual failure is a committee draft where relief, identity and control all
get a line each, and the result is a paragraph that means nothing to anyone.

If you cannot choose, the copy is being asked to do a job that should be split
across two pages.
